ProtoSem · Week 3

Week 3 Computational Hardware & 3D Product Design

From Hardware Fundamentals to Physical Prototyping and 3D CAD Design

Week 3 combined two important areas of engineering learning: understanding computational hardware, electrical and electronics fundamentals, and developing practical 3D design and prototyping skills. The first two days focused on computational hardware, electrical systems, electronic components, power distribution, and motors. The remaining three days focused on design fundamentals, Autodesk Fusion 360, 3D modelling, physical prototyping, and transforming real-world concepts into digital CAD models.

Overview

Day 2

Electrical & Electronics Fundamentals

Focus: Understanding the fundamental components and actuation systems used in electronic and electromechanical systems.
View day details
  • Studied fundamental electronic components and their practical applications.
  • Learned the functions of resistors, capacitors, and inductors.
  • Understood how resistors control current, capacitors store electrical energy, and inductors store energy in magnetic fields.
  • Explored different types of motors including DC motors, AC motors, stepper motors, and servo motors.
  • Understood motors as actuators used in robotics, automation, and electromechanical systems.

03
Day 3

Design Fundamentals & Introduction to 3D Modelling

Focus: Moving from engineering fundamentals into product design and CAD modelling.
View day details
  • Introduced the fundamentals of 3D product design.
  • Learned how physical objects can be represented digitally using CAD software.
  • Introduced Autodesk Fusion 360 and its basic modelling environment.
  • Learned the importance of sketches, dimensions, constraints, and geometric accuracy.
  • Created basic 2D sketches before converting them into 3D models.
  • Explored basic 3D modelling operations such as extrude, revolve, fillet, and other fundamental features.
  • Understood the design workflow: Concept → Sketch → Dimensions & Constraints → 3D Features → Final Model.
04
Day 4

Physical Prototyping & Camera Design

Focus: Transforming a physical clay prototype into a digital 3D CAD model.
View day details
  • Created a physical clay model of a camera as an initial product concept.
  • Used clay to explore the camera's shape, proportions, form, and overall physical appearance.
  • Captured an image of the physical clay camera model.
  • Imported the clay model image into Autodesk Fusion 360 as a reference.
  • Used the imported image to guide the dimensions, proportions, and shape of the digital model.
  • Developed the camera geometry in Fusion 360 based on the physical clay prototype.
  • Added product features and structural details to create the final 3D camera model.
05
Day 5

Paper Rocket & 3D Product Modelling

Focus: Turning physical ideas and real-world objects into digital 3D models.
View day details
  • Participated in a hands-on paper rocket activity where we designed and tested a paper rocket to make it fly.
  • Explored how shape, structure, balance, and aerodynamics influence the flight of a simple paper rocket.
  • Used the physical paper rocket activity as inspiration for digital 3D modelling.
  • Created a 3D model of the paper rocket using Autodesk Fusion 360.
  • Converted the physical concept into a digital representation.
  • Also developed other basic 3D structures and product models such as a water bottle and mechanical components.

Reflection

Week 3 was an important part of my ProtoSem journey because it connected computational hardware and electronics fundamentals with practical engineering design. I learned how electrical systems, electronic components, and motors form the foundation of hardware-based systems.

The second half of the week allowed me to move from theory into practical design. I learned the fundamentals of CAD modelling, created physical prototypes, and explored how real-world objects can be transformed into digital 3D models. The camera activity was particularly valuable because I created a clay prototype first and then used its image as a reference inside Fusion 360 to develop the final 3D camera model.

The paper rocket activity also added a fun hands-on engineering element, where we designed and tested a physical rocket before exploring its 3D digital representation. Overall, Week 3 helped me understand the complete engineering process from fundamentals and experimentation to physical prototyping and digital product development.
