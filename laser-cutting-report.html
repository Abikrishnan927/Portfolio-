Laser Cutting

From vector geometry to a physical spider-shaped design

This page summarizes my practical laser-cutting activity from the source report. It follows the documented workflow using the lab's 1490 CO2 laser cutter and RDWorks V8, without adding unsupported machine or material specifications.

01 · Introduction

Introduction to Laser Cutting

Laser cutting is a digital fabrication process used to cut and engrave materials with a focused laser beam. The activity introduced the basic working principles of a CO2 laser cutter, machine settings, and the safety precautions needed before operation.

Working principle

The laser follows paths defined by the prepared digital design. Its movement and settings determine how deeply and accurately the material is cut or scanned. Speed and cut quality are related: slower movement cuts deeper and can produce higher resolution, while faster movement produces a shallower cut. The report notes that results also vary with material type, thickness, focus position, and environment.

02 · Equipment

Laser Cutting Machine

The report identifies the available machine as a 1490 CO2 Laser Cutter. It records a working area of 1300 x 900 mm and a 150 W laser power.

Machine conditions and safety

The practical notes emphasize ventilation, cooling, air assistance, and electrical safety. These conditions matter because the machine uses a high-power laser and produces fumes during operation.

  • Follow the lab's safety requirements before operating the cutter.
  • Pay attention to ventilation, cooling, air assistance, and electrical safety.
  • Check design paths and settings before starting the machine.

03 · Parameters

Power, speed, focus, and thickness

The report focuses on finding an appropriate power and speed combination for a clean and accurate result. Focus position, material thickness, material type, and the environment also influence the outcome.

PowerControls the energy applied to the cut.SpeedChanges depth, resolution, and cut quality.FocusAffects how the beam meets the material.ThicknessChanges the result and required settings.

04 · Design preparation

Preparing the digital model in RDWorks V8

1Downloaded the chosen image and converted it to DXF format.
2Imported the DXF into RDWorks and checked the geometry.
3Unwrapped and separated the imported geometry into individual sections.
4Created a box around the design area and selected it for three-cycle cutting.
5Set the outer outline for one-cycle cutting and internal parts to Scan.
6Checked the complete design, paths, and settings before fabrication.

05 · Practical procedure

Cutting process

After preparing the design, it was positioned in the RDWorks workspace and the cutting paths were checked. The laser cutter was then prepared for operation and started. The machine followed the paths defined in RDWorks while the laser moved along the outline and internal sections of the design.

Observation

The practical session made the relationship between vector paths, machine settings, and the physical output visible. It also showed how a digital design can be transformed into a real object through computer-controlled manufacturing.

06 · Result

Fabrication outcome

The final output was a detailed spider-shaped laser-cut design with an outer border, internal patterns, and supporting sections. The result demonstrated the complete workflow from preparing digital vector geometry to producing a physical object.

Digital vector design→Physical spider-shaped component
